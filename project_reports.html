Client Report

How did you decide what content and images to use for your site plan?

Honestly, I misinterpreted the initial instructions for the site plan. I thought I was actually making the website, so that led to a few adjustments with the team! I had a lot of success learning to play piano through video game music and thought it would be a fun concept. I designed my logo with the idea of combining music and pixel-style video game art.

How did you communicate with your developer? Was there any issues with communication?

Communication with my developer was very straightforward. She was great about getting back to me in a timely manner and kept me up to date on changes and questions from the team. Overall, it was a great experience playing the client role!

Did your developer meet your expectations for your site?

My expectations were definitely met. I think the website looks cute and well organized. I wanted a little more content, but I found that difficult to express to my "hired" team with the deadline we had. I think I'll try this again later as my own personal project, at my own pace, as I continue learning to code.

What will you do differently next time you hire a team to do a project for you?

I'll go into it with the mindset that "someone else has to be able to understand my notes and bring my idea to life." If I had interpreted the instructions correctly from the beginning, I think my website would have looked a little different. For example, I probably would have come up with a different site plan.

Lead Developer Report

How did you manage your development team and comunicate the tasks for your junior devs?

Since I worked full-time, I made it a goal to review the assignment for the week and give my team their tasks by Wednesday night. That also seemed to be when I heard from my other team leads for this project, so I felt comfortable with that timeline. I worked on the "Home" page for this client. My junior developers were great, and we kept timely communication with each other throughout the project.

How did you communicate with your client? Was there any issues?

Our client was great! He had great ideas and knew exactly what he wanted. He provided plenty of pictures and text, so the website came together really quickly once the CSS was in place. He didn't request any changes and told us he loved the website, so we felt like we did a good job.

How did you handle publishing your clients site and sharing it with the client?

The website was published on time with no issues on our end. Our team met every deadline, so there were never any points where we were scrambling to publish changes. After sharing each week's updates with our client, he told us everything was looking good, and I always checked to see if there were any changes he'd like us to make.

How was your experience with managing your site with GitHub?

GitHub was a little intimidating at first, but I now see that it's fairly straightforward to use! I would definitely use it again whenever it's appropriate.

Junior Developer Report

How did you communicate with your team lead? Was there any issues?

I had no issues with my teams, except for one client who was MIA for most of the project. We were never able to get a site plan from him, so unfortunately we didn't complete a second website. Our first website, however, was a fun concept and came together really well by the end!

Did you know what was expected of you for each site?

Yes, my team lead was a great leader, and I was never left wondering what I needed to do. I would initially check the assigned tasks for my role on Canvas first, and then I'd usually hear from my team lead by Wednesday with what he wanted us to work on that week for the project.

Were you able to contribute to your sites?

Yes, I was assigned the "Game Info" page, and the site plan we referenced was easy to follow. Our team lead admitted that the client's site plan was a little bare and came up with the color scheme and the pictures we could use, but he got everything approved by the client. He also encouraged us to add our own appropriate pictures to our pages. I'm happy with what I contributed.

Did you run into any issues with GitHub and adding your changes to the sites?

Thankfully, I didn't run into any issues with GitHub! It was very easy to use, and I made sure to always include notes describing the changes I submitted.

General Experience and Reflections

What was the most challenging part of this project for you? Were you able to overcome it?

I think the most challenging part of this project was leading a team with my minimal coding experience. After seeing how my first team lead set up his GitHub repository, I felt like I needed to tighten mine up. I made sure to check in with my team for their thoughts throughout the project, and I also used my first team lead as a reference for how my GitHub repository should look—he seemed to know what he was doing!

What did you enjoy most about this project?

I really enjoyed seeing the site plan come to life. After putting all of the HTML together, being able to style the website with CSS was very satisfying. It was rewarding to see everything finally look like a real website.

What would you do differently if you could do the project again?

I would go into it with a little more confidence. I think I did a good job managing my workload, and my teams were great to work with. Now that I have a better understanding of GitHub and web development, I'd feel much more comfortable leading another project.

Do you feel more confident in working on real-world projects after this experience? Explain.

I do feel more confident working on real-world projects after this! Working in GitHub really made this class feel like my homework was actually amounting to something. I'll likely be working with a team later in my career, and this project was great practice for how collaborative development should flow.
